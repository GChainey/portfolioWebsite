## On voice, from the current site

These lines already sound right. Use them as reference, not as copy to repeat.

- "Nothing you build here is used to train anybody's model, ours included."
  A plain promise, with a small turn at the end.
- "Deployed into your tenancy with your identity provider — not a demo that never
  ships." Names the fear and dismisses it.
- "Get a working app folder in minutes — not another login wall before you see
  value." Speaks from the user's side of the screen.
- "Operators who know the process best can't change a step without a ticket and a
  sprint." A specific pain, in the user's own words.

The pattern: concrete over abstract, then a short contrast with what the reader
fears.

## Copy rules

Mechanical rules. An agent should be able to check every one.

### Spelling and grammar

- Australian English: organisation, colour, optimise, licence (noun), license (verb).
- Use the Oxford comma: `TODO` (the site does both today).
- Use contractions: `TODO` (the site does, e.g. "can't", "Let's").

### Casing

- Headings and buttons use sentence case: "Let's improve a process", "Get started".
- Product names keep their own casing (see `concepts.md`).

### Punctuation

- Ranges use an en dash with no spaces: "6–8 weeks".
- Asides use an em dash with spaces: "— not a demo that never ships". `TODO`: confirm.
- No exclamation marks: `TODO`: confirm.

### Numbers

- Numbers under 10 are written as `TODO`. The site uses both "four steps" and "4 blocks".
- Units are abbreviated ("6–8 wks") only in stat tiles.
